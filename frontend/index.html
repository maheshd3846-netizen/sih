VERIFICATION
PHASE 1

Single-Model GFS Baseline

Established 0.25° spatial grid over AP & Telangana. Validated 24h accumulation alignment with IMD gridded rainfall (June 2024, N=23,730, MAE 7.04 mm, RMSE 13.72 mm).

PHASE 2

ECMWF IFS Integration

Ingested ECMWF IFS open-data 0.25° run. Discovered orthogonal errors (r ≈ 0.55). Demonstrated that a 50/50 static ensemble drops MAE to 6.67 mm and RMSE to 11.39 mm (p < 0.0001).

PHASE 3

Adaptive Weighting Audit

Evaluated 7 causal adaptive weighting architectures under strictly causal T-1 latency. Demonstrated that adaptive weighting fails to show statistically significant MAE improvement (p = 0.505).

PHASE 4

Multi-Period Generalization

Expanded across July & August 2024 (N=72,772). Proved 50/50 superiority (p = 0.016) and established the calibrated disagreement confidence engine.

Why two NWP models?

NOAA GFS and ECMWF IFS employ distinct numerical dynamical cores and microphysical convective parameterizations. Their forecast errors over the Indian monsoon region are only moderately correlated (r ≈ 0.55). Combining both models creates an ensemble that cancels out individual spatial biases.

Why a validated 50/50 equal-weight ensemble?

Across the June–August evaluation period, the tested adaptive fusion did not demonstrate a robust MAE advantage over the 50/50 ensemble (50/50 MAE: 7.321 mm vs. adaptive MAE: 7.372 mm, p = 0.016 via paired bootstrap); therefore, equal-weight fusion was retained as the operational strategy. Equal weighting provides maximum robustness against monsoon synoptic variability without incurring parameter instability.

Why model disagreement for confidence?

Historical evaluation found a positive association between model disagreement D = |GFS - ECMWF| and forecast error magnitude (r = 0.4915, ρ = 0.5843). When models agree (D < 0.11 mm), historical MAE is 2.02 mm. When they diverge (D ≥ 2.06 mm), historical MAE rises to 9.46 mm.

Why NO Machine Learning or Neural Networks?

This is a cornerstone of scientific discipline. When causal empirical evaluation proves that tested adaptive weighting does not provide an operational advantage over the static ensemble, adding non-linear neural networks or black-box ML would introduce unjustified complexity and temporal leakage risks without demonstrated benefit.

PROJECT OVERVIEW

About SIH26081 Precipitation Fusion

An operational, research-validated multi-model meteorological intelligence platform developed for Smart India Hackathon.

Mission

To provide disaster management authorities, agricultural planners, and meteorological stakeholders in Andhra Pradesh and Telangana with a dependable, explainable, and scientifically validated rainfall forecast intelligence platform that pairs multi-model consensus with transparent, empirical uncertainty metrics.

Geospatial Domain

Coordinates: 12.0°N to 20.0°N, 76.0°E to 85.0°E
Resolution: 0.25° × 0.25° (~27 km grid cells)
Coverage: 791 verified land cells across Coastal Andhra Pradesh (136 cells), Rayalaseema (224 cells), and Telangana (341 cells).

Data Ingestion Specifications

• NOAA GFS: 0.25° Global Forecast System, 00 UTC cycle, 0–24h APCP accumulation.
• ECMWF IFS: 0.25° Integrated Forecasting System, 00 UTC cycle, 0–24h total precipitation (tp).
• IMD Gridded Rainfall: 0.25° daily gridded binary observation from National Climate Centre, IMD Pune.

Scientific Guardrails

• Zero Fabricated Probabilities: We report empirical confidence classes and validated historical MAE values, never arbitrary percentage probabilities.
• Zero Silent Substitution: Missing source data produces explicit system status codes rather than masked zeroes or synthetic interpolations.
